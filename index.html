Childhood Immunisation in Australia

For years, Australia maintained high childhood immunisation coverage in the world. However, immunisation coverage has started to slip and has never reached the national target of 95%.

This visualisation shows the declining trend in childhood immunisation coverage across age groups, vaccines, states and local areas, how many children are not fully immunised, and why this matters for public health.

Chapter 1

Vaccines have already changed Australia's health story

Measles is one of the most dangerous diseases, and it has affected thousands of Australians in the early 1990s.

As the vaccination program expanded, reported cases decrease significantly, showing the impact of widespread immunisation.

Chapter 6

Vaccine preventable diseases still carry a heavy burden

Immunisation coverage is important since vaccines help prevent serious illnesses and hospitalisations.

These charts explore disease cases, hospital admissions, healthcare costs and the complications these diseases can cause.

Why the surge? The surge may be linked to declining immunisation coverage and reduced exposure during COVID-19 restrictions.

Whooping cough also follows natural outbreak cycles, with cases rising even before the pandemic.

Key takeaways

How far. Coverage has been fallen since 2020 and never reached the 95% target.

Where. At aged two , every state is weakest with Northen Territory and Western Australia being the lowest

How many. 68,159 children were not fully immunised in one year and mostly lived in big cities.

Why it matters. Australia recorded 57,133 whooping cough cases in 2024, showing the burden of vaccine-preventable diseases
